Task 1 · Total LTV

Blended LTV: $136.07 gross, $119.74 net.

Method. Every subscriber pays the intro price (period 0). The share still paying in period n is Sn = C01 × C12 × … × C(n−1)n. Subscription revenue = intro price + recurring price × ΣSn, counting renewals that start within 52 weeks. Upsells = conversion × price, paid once at checkout. Net = Gross × 0.88.

Task 2 · Plan Upgrade A/B model

The upgrade breaks even at 8.3% conversion.

The test only touches 4-week subscribers (70% of the mix). The upgrade offer takes the second upsell's checkout slot. Let x be the upgrade purchase conversion.

Control 4-week gross

19.99 + 21.00 + 3.60 + 95.98 = 140.57

Test 4-week gross

19.99 + 21.00 + x × (49.99 + 89.43) + (1 − x) × 95.98

89.43 is the 12-week renewal stream (4 renewals at $62.99 on the 12-week curve). Each buyer gains 49.99 + 89.43 − 95.98 = +$43.44 gross. Every 4-week subscriber in the test loses the second upsell, worth −$3.60.

Recommendation
Run the test, but treat it as a test of a $49.99 one-time offer, not of the idea that "longer plans are worth more".

Why run it. Break-even (8.3%) is below the 12% this checkout slot already converts at. The worst case (0%) costs ~$2.22 of blended net LTV per subscriber, at 12–20% the upside is +0.8% to +2.6%.

Why the hypothesis fails. Without the fee, an upgrade loses $6.55 per buyer: the 12-week plan's early churn (C01 = 64%, C12 = 57%) is worth less than the 4-week plan's long 80% tail.
